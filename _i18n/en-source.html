Weather for your boat

Green means within your limits. For your boat, not everyone’s.

Set the wind, gust and wave limits that suit your boat and crew. Every hour of the next ten days is rated green, yellow, orange or red against your own limits, with fog, thunderstorms and official warnings counted too. The colours compare; you and your crew decide.

  • Your limits for wind, gusts and waves, boat by boat
  • Radar and lightning (North America and Europe), rain, wind and waves on one map with one slider
  • Storm and wind alerts while you record a trip or watch the anchor (with a data connection)
  • The best hours to leave for your planned route

Automatic trips

Leave the dock. The logbook fills itself in.

Turn on Auto trip once, then no button to remember. Dockbook notices when you leave your marina or anchorage, records the trip in the background, and names it after your saved places or the nearest marina, bay or town.

  • Route, time, distance, speeds, fuel and engine hours for every outing
  • The weather along the way, hour by hour, and the water level when you left
  • An hour-by-hour logbook with your notes, to share as a PDF
  • Your season at a glance: trips, days on the water, who came aboard

Anchor watch

A second pair of eyes on the anchor.

Drop the anchor and set the circle: the wind drifts across the map, so you see what the boat feels. If the boat drags, the alarm sounds, even with the screen locked (keep Dockbook open in the background), and a wind alert can tell you when it blows hard from a side your anchorage is open to. It helps you keep watch; it doesn’t replace one.

  • The wind on the map; “Wind up” turns it so the boat lies below its anchor
  • Going ashore in the tender? One tap pauses the watch
  • GPS smoothing: fewer false alarms at 3 a.m.

Water levels NEW

Know the lake before you leave the dock.

Live levels on the lakes, reservoirs and big rivers boats use in Canada and the United States, compared with last year. On Lake Champlain and the Great Lakes, Dockbook also compares the water with your chart.

  • 7 days, 30 days, the season or the year (U.S. stations), with the usual range where the records allow
  • On Lake Champlain and the Great Lakes, red when the water is below chart zero: less water than the chart shows

Instruments and batteries NEW

Your boat’s instruments, on your iPhone.

Connect Dockbook to your boat’s Wi‑Fi gateway and see your speed, engines, fuel and tanks, wind, depth and water temperature together on one screen. Your trips then log the engine hours and the fuel used on their own, and keep what the instruments read along the way: the shallowest spot, the depth at anchor, the wind and the water temperature. Back at a favourite anchorage, Dockbook remembers the depth from your last visit and adjusts it for today’s water level: “about 6 ft now”.

Lithium batteries with Bluetooth show their charge, the time left and every cell, and warn you before they reach your reserve.

  • Finds the gateway by itself: NMEA 0183 and NMEA 2000 (Yacht Devices, Signal K and other Wi‑Fi gateways)
  • Every engine, tank and battery the boat sends, named your way
  • Your favourite spots remember their depth, visit after visit
  • Volthium batteries and those with a JBD chip, plus JK, Daly and LiTime

Dockbook only reads: it never changes a setting on your gateway or your batteries. For information, not for navigation: always check your depth sounder.

Trip replay NEW

Relive the day. Share it as a 12-second video.

Play the trips Dockbook recorded back on the map with the radar, wind and waves met along the way (radar in Canada and the U.S.), then share them as a short vertical video, ready for social media or the family chat.

  • Your boat in its colours, the weather along the way and a summary at the end
  • Your choice of map: chart, satellite or both

Questions

Before you cast off

Is there a free trial?

Yes: 30 days, risk-free. Tap “Start my 30 free days” to try every feature; nothing is charged when the 30 days end. Afterwards, new entries and trips, the anchor watch and the weather need the yearly subscription, which renews every year until you cancel it. What’s already in your logbook stays readable and exportable either way, and nothing is deleted.

Does Dockbook work without cell coverage?

Yes. Trips, the anchor watch and your logbook work offline. Weather, alerts and charts need a connection, and a trip gets its weather when it’s saved, so save it with a signal if you can.

What boats does Dockbook support?

Powerboats and sailboats, with or without an engine: up to four engines (outboard, sterndrive or inboard; saildrive on a sailboat). A tender or a personal watercraft carried aboard has its own papers and services. You can keep several boats.

Can I use Dockbook on a Wi‑Fi‑only iPad?

Yes, for everything at the dock. A Wi‑Fi‑only iPad has no GPS, so let your iPhone record the trips and watch the anchor; the trips then appear on the iPad through iCloud. To record on the iPad itself, connect a Bluetooth GPS.

Can I bring in my existing logbook?

Yes. Import trips from GPX files, your records from a spreadsheet (CSV), or a whole boat from the PDF your previous boating app exports. Dockbook matches them to your boat.

Where is my data?

On your devices, and in your own iCloud account if you choose Sync with iCloud. No ads, no tracking, no account to create, and you can back everything up whenever you like (your logbook, and a second file with the trips’ weather replays). Read the privacy policy

Is it a navigation app?

No. Dockbook is a logbook and planner: its charts, weather and alerts are for planning, not navigation. Always carry official charts and all the safety equipment required for your boat and waters, check the official marine forecast, and keep a watch.

Does it work outside Canada and the U.S.?

Yes: the logbook, automatic trips, anchor watch, maintenance and costs work anywhere, with forecasts worldwide (Apple Weather) and ocean waves at sea. In Europe, the map also has the radar, the rain forecast of Germany’s weather service and lightning. Official charts, water levels and the safety-gear rules are for Canada and the U.S.; elsewhere, OpenSeaMap shows buoys and markers.
